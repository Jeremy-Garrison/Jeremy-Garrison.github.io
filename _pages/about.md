I am a Visiting Assistant Professor at the College of the Holy Cross. I hold a doctorate and a master's degree in Economics from [Colorado State University](https://economics.colostate.edu/) and bachelor's degrees in Environmental Policy and Mathematics from [the University of Tulsa](https://utulsa.edu/). 

In my research, I employ evolutionary game theory and cross-cultural evidence to explore questions of social and cultural economics and political economy, drawing on anthropology, evolutionary biology, and psychology as well as economics. In particular, I study the social, cultural, and behavioral effects of economic systems. In my theoretical work, I develop evolutionary models of endogenous preferences. My current empirical work uses folklore and ethnographic text as data and a meta-analysis of economic experiments to investigate the social and cultural consequences of markets and capitalism. In the future, I aim to continue my research on the evolution of cooperation and to extend my methods to analyzing human-environment interactions and systems of power and oppression. More information can be found on my [research](research) page.
